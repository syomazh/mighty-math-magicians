STEP 2

Rotation classes have p-power size

Rotate the p children of any node. A node is symmetric (S) if its p subtrees are equivalent, asymmetric (A) if not; d(T) counts the A-nodes.

Here d(T) = 2: four trees. The root is symmetric although its subtrees differ; they are rotations of each other.

Lemma: a class has exactly p to the d(T) elements. Induction at the root: since p is prime, the p rotations of the word of child classes are either all equal (symmetric root) or all different (asymmetric root, one extra factor p). Rotations preserve leaf depths, so the trees from Step 1 split into classes, and C is a sum of powers of p.
