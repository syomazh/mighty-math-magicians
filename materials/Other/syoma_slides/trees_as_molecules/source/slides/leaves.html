Leaves are the hydrogens on carbon: 2n + 1

IN THE PROOF · EQUATION (LEAVES)

“Replacing a leaf of a plane r-ary tree by an internal node with r new leaves increases the leaf count by r − 1 … Hence ℓ(T) = 1 + (r − 1)·#{internal nodes of T}.”

3 → 5 → 7 leaves: each new node (amber) eats one leaf and brings three.

IN CHEMISTRY · THE FORMULA CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH

With r = 3: n carbons carry 2n + 1 hydrogens (the H of OH is not counted). That is why chemists write alcohols as CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH.

methanol CH₃OH · 3 H

ethanol C₂H₅OH · 5 H

propanol C₃H₇OH · 7 H

butanol C₄H₉OH · 9 H

So s₃(2n + 1) in our theorem is the digit sum of the number of hydrogens on carbon, written in base 3.
