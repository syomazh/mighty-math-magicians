## The mathematical model (so that you can judge whether a source really matches)

A rooted ternary tree: every internal node has exactly 3 ordered children; leaves are unlabelled. Read it as an alkyl group R attached to something X that has no symmetry of its own: internal node = carbon, leaf = hydrogen, the edge to the parent = the bond to the rest of the molecule, the 3 children = the other three substituents of that carbon. With n carbons there are m = 2n + 1 hydrogens.

- A rotation at a node cyclically permutes its 3 child subtrees (a turn of that group by 120 degrees about its bond to the parent). Trees equivalent under rotations at any nodes form a class; the classes are the alkyl groups with stereoisomers distinguished (OEIS A000625). Reflections (exchanging two children) are NOT allowed: they give the mirror-image group.
- A node is symmetric if its three child subtrees are equivalent, asymmetric otherwise. A class with d asymmetric nodes contains 3^d ordered trees (its staggered conformations), and its symmetry group (the rotations that fix the tree) has order 3^(n-d).
- That group permutes the leaves (hydrogens); its orbits are the sets of hydrogens that internal rotations interchange.
- Atoms: complete ternary trees with 3^j leaves: H (1), methyl (3 H), tert-butyl (9 H), tri-tert-butylmethyl (27 H). In such a group all 3^j hydrogens are one orbit.
- Theorem of the team (proved): the largest symmetry group for n carbons has order 3^v, v the exponent of 3 in m!; the groups attaining it are built from atoms according to the base-3 digits of m; and the number of ordered trees, binom(3n, n)/(2n+1), is divisible by 3 exactly to the power n - v.

Already established with sources (do not redo): OEIS A000625; OEIS A121179 (staggered conformers of alkyls, from papers of Cyvin and co-authors that nobody opened); Domalski and Hearing 1988, J. Phys. Chem. Ref. Data 17, 1637, Appendix D (internal symmetry number 3 per symmetric terminal rotor); Gilson and Irikura 2010; Flory's Nobel lecture (three rotational isomeric states). See `references/README.md`, entries 11, 38, 39, 52, 53.
