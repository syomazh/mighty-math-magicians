# W023 — Lean: the lemma "Normal form" for rotation equivalence

## Goal

In the Lean project `lean/`, rotation equivalence of `p`-ary trees is *defined* in a normal form (`MMM.PTree.Rot` in `lean/MMM/Trees.lean`): two nodes are equivalent when, after a cyclic shift of the children of the first, the children are equivalent one by one. The paper defines the relation differently: as the equivalence relation *generated by* rotating the children of one node, anywhere in the tree, by one position. Prove in Lean that the two agree.

## Deliverable (exact statements, in `namespace MMM.PTree`)

```lean
/-- One rotation: the children of one node, anywhere in the tree, are shifted cyclically by
one position. -/
inductive RotStep {p : ℕ} [NeZero p] : PTree p → PTree p → Prop
  | here (f : Fin p → PTree p) : RotStep (node f) (node fun i => f (i + 1))
  | below (f : Fin p → PTree p) (i : Fin p) (T : PTree p) (h : RotStep (f i) T) :
      RotStep (node f) (node (Function.update f i T))

theorem rot_iff_eqvGen {p : ℕ} [NeZero p] (S T : PTree p) :
    Rot S T ↔ Relation.EqvGen RotStep S T
```

Small adjustments to make `RotStep` elaborate are allowed (for example where the `NeZero` argument goes), but the meaning must be exactly this and the theorem must be exactly this equivalence. If you must change anything, say so in the report.

## Suggested proof

- (⇐) `RotStep S T → Rot S T` by induction on the step: `here` is `Rot.node` with shift 1 and `Rot.refl` on the children; `below` is `Rot.node` with shift 0, the induction hypothesis at child `i`, `Rot.refl` at the others. Then `EqvGen` follows because `Rot` is reflexive, symmetric and transitive.
- (⇒) by induction on `Rot S T`. For `Rot.node f g r h`: first rotate the root `r` times (iterate `here`; induct on `r.val`), reaching `node fun i => f (i + r)`; then replace the children one at a time, each replacement being a lift of `EqvGen RotStep (f (i + r)) (g i)` through `below` (prove: `EqvGen RotStep A B → EqvGen RotStep (node (Function.update F i A)) (node (Function.update F i B))`), for example by induction over a `Finset (Fin p)` of the positions already replaced.
