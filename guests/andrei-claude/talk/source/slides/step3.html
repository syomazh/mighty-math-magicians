STEP 3

A lower bound from base-p digit sums

Claim: d(T) ≥ t(m) = (sₚ(m) − 1)/(p − 1) for every tree T with m leaves. Induction on m, splitting at the root.

Symmetric root. Its p subtrees are equivalent, so m = p·m₁ and d(T) = p·d(T₁) ≥ p·t(m₁) ≥ t(m₁) = t(m), with equality exactly when d(T) = 0, i.e. T is a complete p-ary tree.

Asymmetric root. If the subtrees have m₁, …, mₚ leaves, then d(T) = 1 + Σ d(Tᵢ) ≥ 1 + Σ t(mᵢ) ≥ t(m), with equality exactly when d(Tᵢ) = t(mᵢ) for each i and the mᵢ add without carries.

This is the divisibility Konvalinka obtains from a larger group action.

Two facts about base-p addition, no factorials: multiplying by p keeps the digit sum, and the digit sum is subadditive, with the defect equal to p − 1 times the number of carries. The induction has two cases. A symmetric root multiplies the count by p, which can only help. An asymmetric root costs exactly one, and the digit sums pay for it unless there are carries. Summing class sizes p to the d(T), every term is divisible by p to the t.
