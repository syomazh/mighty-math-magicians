THE PROBLEM

How often does p divide the tree count?

Known: Konvalinka (2007), via Legendre’s formula for the power of p in N!. Problem 137: prove it from the symmetries of trees, without factorial valuations.

Mighty Math Magicians · Caltech Mathathon 2026

The Fuss–Catalan number counts planar q-ary trees with n internal nodes; such a tree has m = (q−1)n+1 leaves. Write m in base p with digits m_j between 0 and p − 1, and let s_p(m) be the digit sum. When q is a power of p, the exact power of p dividing the count is t = (s_p(m) − 1)/(p − 1). For p = q = 2 this is the classical fact that the 2-adic valuation of the Catalan number C_n is s_2(n+1) − 1; for example C_6 = 132 = 4·33. We also get the residue of the quotient mod p. Konvalinka proved this with factorial valuations; the problem asks for a proof from tree symmetries.
