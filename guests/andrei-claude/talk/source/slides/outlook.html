Where this could go. Replace the number of trees by a polynomial: each tree gets the weight x to its height times y to the sum of its leaf depths. A rotation does not change the depth of any leaf, so the weight is constant on a rotation class, and Steps 2 and 3 apply word for word: every coefficient is a sum of class sizes, so it is divisible by p to the t, and modulo p to the t + 1 only minimal classes contribute. Example by hand: q = 3, n = 3, so m = 7 = 21 in base 3 and t = 1. There are 12 trees of two shapes. Left: the root has two internal children; the leaf can be in any of 3 places, so 3 trees, one rotation class; height 2, leaf depths 1 + 6 times 2 = 13. These are the minimal trees: a leaf and two cherries under the root. Right: a chain; 3 places for the internal child times 3 for the internal grandchild, so 9 trees, one rotation class; height 3, leaf depths 2 + 4 + 9 = 15. So P_3 = 3 x^2 y^13 + 9 x^3 y^15: both coefficients are divisible by 3, and modulo 9 only the minimal class is left. Larger example, by computer: q = 3, n = 6, m = 13 = 111 in base 3, t = 1. A minimal tree is a root carrying complete subtrees with 1, 3 and 9 leaves: two cyclic orders, three trees each, six trees, all of height 3 and leaf depth sum 1 + 6 + 27 = 34. So the whole polynomial, 1428 trees and 12 monomials, is 6 x^3 y^34 modulo 9. Status: the divisibility follows from our lemmas. The general formula for the residue (a cherry collapse with exact depths) is checked by computer in 29 cases, and its proof is only a sketch. The same proof works for any statistic that rotations preserve. More interesting are statistics that a rotation changes in a controlled way. Example: for Gaussian binomial coefficients, weight a lattice path by x to the sum of the positions of its east steps. Rotating a block multiplies the weight by a power of x, so the sum over a rotation class is a geometric sum, and it vanishes modulo a cyclotomic polynomial. The result is one cyclotomic factor for each carry, in place of one factor p. Caveat: we checked the cyclotomic factors by computer, and a group-action proof of the related congruence, the q-Lucas theorem, is in the literature; we have not proved this ourselves, and exactness by symmetry is open as far as we found. Statistics that depend on the order of the children with no such control, such as area or peaks, have coefficients that are not all divisible by p to the t.
