STEP 5

Collapse the complete subtrees

A minimal tree is a skeleton of t asymmetric nodes carrying complete subtrees, the atoms. Since leaf counts add without carries, there are exactly mⱼ atoms of height j, where mⱼ is the j-th base-p digit of m: sₚ(m) atoms in all.

Nmin = |S(m, k)|: skeletons with mⱼ leaves labelled j, children taken up to cyclic rotation at every node; a leaf labelled j sits at depth ≡ −j (mod k).

q = 9 (p = 3, k = 2), n = 2, m = 17 = 122₃: atoms of sizes 9, 3, 3, 1, 1; root adds 3 + 3 + 11 = 010₃ + 010₃ + 102₃ with no carry. Depth condition: labels 1 sit at odd depth, labels 0 and 2 at even depth. This is the only skeleton; without the condition (k = 1) the same atoms give 10.

Unwinding the characterization: maximal complete subtrees are atoms, the remaining nodes form a skeleton of t asymmetric nodes. Carry-free addition means digit vectors just add, so the atoms are exactly the digits of m. Conversely any skeleton on the digit atoms is minimal. Atoms are rotation invariant, so minimal classes are cyclic skeletons. Here q = 9, so k = 2: a 9-ary node is two levels of the ternary tree, and every leaf of the big tree is at even depth. An atom of height j must therefore have its root at depth congruent to −j mod 2: the two cherries (height 1) at depth 1, the two single leaves (height 0) and the 9-leaf atom (height 2) at depth 2. That forces the skeleton: there is exactly one. With k = 1, for q = 3 and n = 8, which has the same m = 17, there are 10.
