Fixed trees are cherries

σ fixes a tree exactly when the labels x₁, …, xₚ form a cherry, and its action is one of the p − 1 non-trivial powers of the rotation of that cherry. That gives p − 1 possible labellings of the cherry’s leaves by x₁, …, xₚ.

Collapsing the cherry removes p leaves of class c and adds one leaf of class c + 1 (mod k), one level closer to the root. Here three leaves of class 0 at depth 2 become one leaf z of class 1 at depth 1. Four of the 8 fixed trees are shown.

Mighty Math Magicians · Caltech Mathathon 2026

Why cherries: take the lowest common ancestor of the cycled labels. The induced symmetry must rotate its children nontrivially, which forces every child to be one of the cycled leaves. The p − 1 compatible cyclic orders are Petersen’s count from his proof of Wilson’s theorem. Collapsing the cherry to one new leaf one level up gives a label of class c + 1. So each collapse multiplies the count by −1 mod p. In the picture, k = 2 and a = (6, 1): the 8 fixed trees (the other four have y2 and y3 exchanged) collapse in pairs onto the 4 trees of T(3, 2), which is the example of Step 6 and the start of the chain on the next slide. So 80 ≡ 8 = 2 · 4 ≡ −4 mod 3.
