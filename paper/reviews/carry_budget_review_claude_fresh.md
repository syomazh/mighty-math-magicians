# Referee report: "The carry budget: Step 2 and Step 3 of the main proof from one identity"

## Verdict

The mathematics of the note is correct. Fact 1, Fact 2, Fact 3, Consequence 1 and the two-bullet criterion of Consequence 2 (including the induction for completeness) are proved as written, and I could not break them by hand or by computer: about 1.36 million rotation classes checked exhaustively for p = 2, 3, 5, 7, plus random trees with up to millions of leaves for p up to 13, with zero violations. Nothing that the proof uses is wrong. The defects are at the edges: the "built from the digits of m" description and the converse of the skeleton picture are asserted but never derived; Consequence 3 (symmetry group, Sylow) is asserted with no definition and no proof (it is true; a proof is supplied below); "number of carries" silently means "amount carried", which matters for every p ≥ 3; and one sentence of the counter analogy is off by one.

What I could not check, because I was told to read only the note: that the statements coincide with Propositions "Lower bound" and "Local characterization" and "the digit lemma" of `paper/main.tex`; the script `experiments/E011_crossfield_checks/excess.py`; "Finding 4" (sand) and the "accounting proof" mentioned in the last section.

## Findings, ranked by severity

1. **GAP** — Consequence 2, last sentence of the paragraph (line 48: "So a minimal tree is a skeleton of carry-free asymmetric nodes whose leaves are complete trees"), and spoken version, last sentence (line 54: "The trees that waste no carry are the ones built from the digits of m").
   Four things are missing between the two bullets and these sentences.
   (a) The induction shows that the subtree below a *child* of a symmetric node is complete. That the symmetric node itself is complete (its children are complete with the same leaf count p^j, hence the same height) is not said.
   (b) "Skeleton" presupposes that the asymmetric nodes form a connected top part of the tree, i.e. that no asymmetric node lies below a symmetric one. This follows from (a), since every node of a complete tree is symmetric, but it is not said.
   (c) The skeleton sentence is stated only in the direction minimal ⟹ skeleton. The converse is true but not stated: a carry-free node is automatically asymmetric (Fact 2 says a symmetric node carries), and every node of a complete tree is symmetric with children of p^i leaves, so both bullets hold.
   (d) "Built from the digits of m" is nowhere derived in the note. Nothing in the note says which complete trees occur or how many. Also the note never shows that a tree with d = t exists at all, which the word "minimal" presupposes.
   *Fix.* Replace the sentence by the following statement and proof. Write m = Σ a_j p^j in base p and s = s_p(m).
   **Claim.** T is minimal (d = t) if and only if T is obtained from a p-ary tree S with s leaves by replacing the leaves of S by complete trees, exactly a_j of them of height j. Every S and every placement works; the nodes of S are then exactly the asymmetric nodes of T (there are (s−1)/(p−1) = t of them). In particular minimal trees exist for every m.
   *Proof.* (⇐) For a node v of S let n_j(v) be the number of atoms of height j below v. Then n_j(v) ≤ a_j ≤ p−1, so Σ_j n_j(v) p^j is the base-p expansion of m_v, and n_j(v) = Σ_i n_j(v_i): the digits add column by column, so c(v) = 0, and by Fact 2 v is asymmetric. Inside an atom every node is symmetric with children of p^i leaves. Both bullets hold, so d = t. (⇒) By the bullets and the induction of the note, every symmetric node is complete; hence every node below a symmetric node is symmetric, the asymmetric nodes form a top part S of the tree, and what hangs below S are complete trees (leaves included, height 0). Carry-free addition at every node of S means the j-th digit of m_v is the sum of the j-th digits of the m_(v_i); descending from the root, the j-th digit of m is the number of atoms of height j. ∎
   Checked by computer in both directions (see "Part A" below): for every m in range, the set of classes built this way equals the set of classes with d = t.

2. **UNCLEAR** — Setting, "Carries at a node" (lines 12–16: "The number of carries made is c(v) = …"); also Fact 2 (line 32), Consequence 1 (line 42) and the spoken version (line 54: "you make the same number of carries").
   With p summands one column can pass up to p−1 to the next column. c(v) as defined by the formula is the **total amount carried** (the sum of the carry digits), not the number of columns that carry and not the number of carry events. The two agree only for p = 2. Example, p = 3, a symmetric node with three children of 5 leaves: 12 + 12 + 12 = 120 in base 3; the units column passes 2, the next column passes 1; c(v) = 3 = s_3(5), but only two columns carry. Under the "columns" reading Fact 1 and Fact 2 are both false: for the 15-leaf ternary tree with three copies of the 5-leaf tree, ν(15) = 6 but only 5 columns carry in total. An asymmetric example: p = 5, a node adding 1 + 1 + 1 + 9 + 9 = 21 passes 2 out of the units column. The note's own justification ("each carry replaces p units in one column by 1 unit in the next") does define a carry as a unit trade, so nothing is wrong, but a listener who thinks "a column either carries or not" will find Fact 2 false at the first ternary example.
   *Fix.* Line 12–14: "The amount carried (each trade of p units in one column for 1 unit in the next counts as one carry; with p summands one column can make up to p−1 of them) is c(v) = …". In the talk, say once: "a carry is one trade of p for 1; a column can make several".

3. **GAP** — Consequence 3 (line 50). Three assertions are made and none is proved in the note: (i) "the symmetry group of a tree" is "a p-group of order p^σ"; (ii) it acts "faithfully on the m leaves"; (iii) "Minimal trees are exactly the trees whose symmetry group is a Sylow p-subgroup". The symmetry group is not even defined in the note. The note says these are not needed for the proof, which is right, but as written they are bare assertions that a judge can ask about. All three are true under the natural definition; here is the missing argument.
   *Fix.* Definition: a rotation isomorphism between leaves is the identity; a rotation isomorphism T → T' between nodes is a shift r together with rotation isomorphisms T_(i+r) → T'_i for all i; Aut(T) is the group of rotation isomorphisms T → T. (i) For r = 0 there are Π|Aut(T_i)| automorphisms. For r ≠ 0 there are any only if T_(i+r) ~ T_i for all i, and since p is prime r generates Z/p, so this happens exactly when the root is symmetric; then each shift again contributes Π|Aut(T_i)| (the isomorphisms T_(i+r) → T_i, when there is one, are as many as the automorphisms of T_i). So |Aut(T)| = p^[root symmetric] · Π|Aut(T_i)| = p^σ by induction. (This is the only place in the note where primality of p is used.) (ii) An automorphism permutes the vertices; a vertex is determined by the set of leaves below it; so an automorphism fixing all leaves is the identity. (iii) Hence Aut(T) is a subgroup of S_m of order p^σ, so σ ≤ v_p(m!) = ν(m) (Legendre), with equality iff Aut(T) is a Sylow p-subgroup; and σ = ν(m) iff d = t by Fact 3.
   Checked by computer on 7,004 classes (see below). If the remark stays, "Legendre" needs an entry in the bibliography file; it is cited by name only.

4. **UNCLEAR** — spoken version (line 54): "and what is left over, t nodes, must be asymmetric". Taken literally this says every tree has exactly t asymmetric nodes, which is false: the binary caterpillar with 4 leaves has d = 2 and t = 0; the ternary tree (∗∗(∗∗(∗∗∗))) with 7 leaves has d = 2 and t = 1.
   *Fix.* "…so at least t nodes are left over, and those must be asymmetric."

5. **WRONG** (minor; analogy only, no effect on any proof) — "Relation to the other pictures", Counter (line 58): "t is the potential still stored in the digits of m, in units of one carry". The potential stored in the digits of m is s_p(m), which is s_p(m)/(p−1) carries; t is (s_p(m) − 1)/(p−1). Off by 1/(p−1): for p = 2, m = 1 the stored potential is 1 and t = 0.
   *Fix.* "t is the potential gained while counting from 1 (a single leaf) to m, in units of one carry: t = (s_p(m) − s_p(1))/(p−1). The m − 1 increments supply N = (m−1)/(p−1) units; ν(m) are spent on carries, t remain stored."

6. **GAP** (trivial) — Fact 2 (line 28): "If v is symmetric, its children all have the same number b of leaves". This uses that rotation-equivalent trees have the same number of leaves, which is not stated.
   *Fix.* Add "(rotation-equivalent trees have the same number of leaves, by induction on the definition)".

7. **UNCLEAR** — Status line (line 5): "all 1,978 rotation classes of p-ary trees with up to 13–19 leaves, p = 2, 3, 5, 7". The count is right: I reproduce 1,978 = 1,833 + 123 + 15 + 7 with the limits 13, 15, 17, 19 leaves for p = 2, 3, 5, 7 respectively; say so explicitly. But this range barely tests the note for odd p. Among those 1,978 classes, a symmetric node with s_p(b) > 1 (the first sum of Fact 3) occurs in 222 binary classes, in exactly one ternary class, and in no class for p = 5 or 7 (the first ones need 45 and 91 leaves); a column carrying more than 1 (finding 2) occurs in exactly one class. The `computed` grade is therefore essentially a p = 2 test of the first sum.
   *Fix.* State the limits per prime, and extend the range or add random large trees (my runs below do both and find no violation).

## Checked and found correct

- **Setting.** N = (m−1)/(p−1); t = N − ν(m); t and ν(m) are integers because s_p(m) ≡ m ≡ 1 (mod p−1). c(v) ≥ 0 and the digit-sum formula for the amount carried.
- **Fact 1, telescoping.** Each non-root vertex appears once with + (in its parent's term); each node appears once with − (in its own term); non-root nodes cancel; what remains is +1 per leaf and −s_p(m) for the root. Correct. (For m = 1 the tree has no node, the sum is empty and ν(1) = 0; the sentence of the proof does not literally cover this case, the statement holds.)
- **Fact 2.** c(v) = (p·s_p(b) − s_p(pb))/(p−1) = s_p(b) because s_p(pb) = s_p(b); s_p(b) ≥ 1 because b ≥ 1. Correct. The parenthesis "every unit of every digit is carried once" is accurate: column j receives p·b_j + b_(j−1), keeps b_(j−1) and passes b_j.
- **Fact 3.** d = N − σ, t = N − ν(m), hence d − t = ν(m) − σ; splitting Σ_v c(v) over symmetric and asymmetric nodes and using Fact 2 gives the right-hand side; every term is ≥ 0. Correct.
- **Consequence 1.** d ≥ t. Correct, and the spoken form of it in line 42 is correct.
- **Consequence 2, the two bullets.** A sum of non-negative terms vanishes iff each term does, so "d = t exactly when" both bullets hold is an exact equivalence, proved in both directions. Both bullets are needed: the binary tree ((∗(∗∗))(∗(∗∗))) (6 leaves, d = 2, t = 1) satisfies bullet 1 only; the binary caterpillar (∗(∗(∗∗))) (4 leaves, d = 2, t = 0) satisfies bullet 2 only.
- **Consequence 2, the induction.** For a child w of a symmetric node in a minimal tree, m_w = p^j. j = 0: leaf. j > 0: w is a node with s_p(m_w) = 1; if it were asymmetric it would be carry-free (bullet 1), so s_p(m_w) = Σ s_p(m_(w_i)) ≥ p > 1, contradiction; so w is symmetric, its children have b leaves with pb = p^j, they are children of a symmetric node, so complete by induction, of equal height. The induction is sound.
- **Consequence 3.** ν(m) = v_p(m!) (Legendre) is correct. The three group statements are true (finding 3) but not proved in the note. "Neither statement is needed for the proof" is correct: Facts 1–3 and Consequences 1–2 use no factorial.
- **Spoken version.** Sentences 1–3 are correct with the reading of "carry" in finding 2; sentence 4 needs "at least" (finding 4); sentence 5 is true but not derived in the note (finding 1).
- **Counter analogy, first sentence.** Counting from 0 to m in base p makes ν(m) carries. Correct.
- **Where primality is used.** Not in Facts 1–3 or Consequences 1–2: they hold verbatim for any arity p ≥ 2 with base-p digits (confirmed for arity 4 and 6 below). Primality enters only in Consequence 3 (order p^σ) and in "a class has p^d trees", which is outside this note.
