| 5 | **Where we stand against prior work.** For p = 2 the story starts in 1939: **Etherington** defined the "mutability" of a binary tree shape (our d), noted that a shape has 2^mutability ordered versions, wrote the generating function with a second variable for it (the p = 2 case of the equation in our Frobenius proof) and showed that the number of leaves is a sum of mutability + 1 powers of two (which gives our lower bound for p = 2). He did not look at divisibility. The orbit argument with the exact valuation for p = 2 is Deutsch–Sagan (and Postnikov–Sagan, weighted), and the minimal trees were found again by biologists (Kersting–Fischer 2021); Konvalinka uses the full symmetric group and gets divisibility only; Gao–Gu and Krattenthaler–Müller are algebraic (the latter expand the generating function in the series Σ x^(q^i), which is our series of atoms). Not found in those texts: the p-group action for q = p^k, the digit skeleton with its depth condition, the carry–cherry count. | A worker read four full texts; this is **not** a novelty certificate. The professor's view is needed. A web check on Saturday (keyword searches of OpenAlex, arXiv, the OEIS; `notes/2026-11-14_crossfield_literature.md`, item 8) found no proof of the valuation by a Frobenius lift or δ-rings, nothing linking it to heaps, counters or brackets, and nothing reading tree symmetries p-adically. It did confirm that the modulo-p starting point of finding 6 (the series Σ z^(p^n) and the relation Φ^p − Φ + z ≡ 0) is in Kauers–Krattenthaler–Müller (2012) and Krattenthaler–Müller (2025); they are now credited. | 10 | **Cross-field readings, ranked** (new). (a) *Statistical physics:* Z_m(y) = Σ over classes of y^d gives the atoms at y = 0, the isomer count at y = 1 and the Fuss–Catalan number at y = p; p-adically y = p is small, so the valuation is the ground-state energy and the residue counts the ground states. (d) *Group theory:* a tree is minimal exactly when its symmetry group is a Sylow p-subgroup of the symmetric group on its leaves.
